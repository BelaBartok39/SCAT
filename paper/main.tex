\begin{abstract}
Knowledge about the security of radio-frequency (RF) links is fragmented
across communities that rarely share vocabulary: electronic warfare,
satellite systems engineering, physical-layer security, and mission cyber
defence. Existing reference knowledge bases operate either at the mission
and spacecraft level or at the enterprise-network level, leaving the
signal itself---its band, waveform, antenna, propagation environment, and
the attacks and mitigations that attach to each---without a navigable
structure. We present Penumbra, an interactive relational taxonomy in
which eleven signal-security facets are crossed with one another to yield
a matrix of relation types, rendered on a rotatable cylindrical surface
whose wrapped axis encodes the countermeasure lifecycle. Three coupled
drill-down views---a ground-to-orbit transmission-security diorama, a
device-level kill-chain scene, and a waveform laboratory---are projections
of the same underlying entity graph, so that a relation selected in one
view can be opened in another. Cell colour encodes evidence density rather
than identity, which makes the surface simultaneously a reference and a
map of where the literature is thin. We describe the taxonomy structure,
the visual encoding and its rejected alternatives, the provenance
discipline governing ingest of external knowledge bases, and an evaluation
plan.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{T}{he} security of a radio link is decided by properties
that no single research community owns. Whether a transmission can be
detected depends on its waveform and its antenna pattern; whether it can
be exploited depends on its framing and its encryption; whether it can be
denied depends on the geometry between transmitter, receiver, and
adversary. These properties are studied in electronic warfare, in
satellite systems engineering, in physical-layer security, and in mission
cyber defence, and each community has its own vocabulary, its own
canonical threats, and its own reference documents.

The consequence is practical. An analyst who wants to know which antenna
architectures are commonly paired with a given protocol, or which
waveforms in deployed use actually achieve a low probability of detection,
or which mitigations have been demonstrated against follower jamming,
must assemble the answer from sources that do not cite one another. A
student encountering the field has no structure into which individual
facts can be placed.

\guide{Ground this paragraph in a concrete anecdote you can defend---the
Viasat/AcidRain 2022 event, the Baltic GNSS interference campaign, or the
public reverse-engineering of Starlink downlink framing. One real incident
that required knowledge from three of the four communities is worth more
than a general claim about fragmentation.}

Existing structured knowledge bases do not fill this gap, because they
were built for adjacent problems. SPARTA \needcite{} catalogues
adversary tactics against space systems at the mission and spacecraft
level. MITRE ATT\&CK \needcite{} and CAPEC \needcite{} operate on
enterprise and application software. Surveys of satellite security
\needcite{} organise the field into segments---space, ground, link, user---
but treat the link segment as a single element rather than a structure.
None of them can answer a question that crosses two signal-domain
properties, because none of them represents signal-domain properties as
first-class entities that can be crossed.

We take the position that the missing artefact is \emph{relational}. The
useful unit of knowledge in this domain is not a fact about a band or a
fact about a modulation, but a fact about the relationship between a band
and a modulation---or between an antenna and a protocol, or between an
attack and a mitigation. A taxonomy of RF signal security should therefore
be a structure over pairs, not a list of items.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A relational taxonomy of RF signal security comprising eleven
        facets crossed with one another, yielding fifty-five distinct
        relation types, of which one---band crossed with security
        objective---is instantiated in full depth as an existence proof.
  \item A visual encoding for facet-by-facet relations on a cyclic
        surface, in which hue carries facet family, luminance carries
        evidence density, and a fixed pair readout replaces
        header-tracing. We report the alternatives we rejected and why.
  \item An interactive instrument comprising four coupled views over a
        single entity graph, in which a relation selected in one view can
        be opened in the others.
  \item A curated crosswalk from mission-level space adversary techniques
        to physical-layer signal facets, together with a provenance
        discipline for ingesting third-party knowledge bases
        reproducibly.
\end{enumerate}

\guide{Contribution 4 is the one most likely to be questioned as
derivative. Defend it as \emph{a mapping nobody has published}, not as
\emph{we imported SPARTA}. If the crosswalk ends up smaller than about
twenty-five rows, demote it from a contribution to a subsection.}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Structured Adversary Knowledge Bases}
SPARTA \needcite{} is the closest reference artefact in the space domain,
enumerating tactics and techniques against spacecraft, ground segments,
and their interfaces. Its unit of analysis is the mission: a technique
describes what an adversary does to a system. ATT\&CK \needcite{} and
CAPEC \needcite{} play the equivalent role for enterprise software and
application-layer attack patterns respectively.

These knowledge bases are complementary to ours rather than competitive,
and the boundary is worth stating precisely: they enumerate adversary
behaviour, whereas we structure the signal-domain properties that
determine whether such behaviour is feasible. A technique such as
``intercept the downlink'' appears in SPARTA as an action; in our
taxonomy the corresponding entry is the set of relations between the
band, the modulation, the antenna geometry, and the framing that jointly
decide whether interception succeeds.

\guide{This paragraph is the paper's most important defensive move. A
reviewer who works on SPARTA will read the paper looking for evidence that
you are re-labelling their work. Make the distinction operational, not
rhetorical: give one example where our structure yields an answer SPARTA
structurally cannot.}

\subsection{Surveys and Taxonomies of Satellite and PHY Security}
Tedeschi \emph{et al.} \needcite{} and Ben Yahia \emph{et al.}
\needcite{} survey satellite communication security and decompose systems
into segments. Pavur \emph{et al.} \needcite{} demonstrate large-scale
cleartext recovery from commercial satellite downlinks; Humphreys
\emph{et al.} \needcite{} characterise LEO downlink framing from
third-party observation. Physical-layer security research \needcite{}
supplies the objective vocabulary---low probability of detection,
interception, and exploitation; anti-jam; traffic-flow security---that we
adopt as one of our eleven facets.

Two limitations recur. First, these treatments are largely
orbit-agnostic, which understates how strongly the threat model depends
on regime. Second, they are organised as prose surveys, so the knowledge
they contain cannot be queried along an axis the author did not
anticipate.

\subsection{Matrix and Adjacency Visualization}
Our central view is an adjacency matrix over a facet set. Matrix
representations are known to outperform node-link diagrams for dense
graphs on most topology-reading tasks, while losing to them on path
following \needcite{}. Since our analytic tasks are lookup and
comparison rather than path following, the matrix is the appropriate
base representation.

The design-structure-matrix and $N^2$-chart traditions in systems
engineering \needcite{} supply the specific convention we adopt: a square
matrix whose rows and columns are the same list, so that every cell is a
relation between two elements of one set.

\guide{You need one or two citations for 3D and curved matrix layouts,
and you should expect a reviewer to ask whether the cylinder is
gratuitous. Search terms: ``curved matrix layout'', ``3D adjacency
matrix'', ``matrix cube''. If the honest answer is that prior work found
3D matrix layouts \emph{worse}, cite it and answer it directly in
Section~IV-B rather than hoping it is missed.}

\section{Taxonomy Structure}

\subsection{Facets}
The taxonomy is built from eleven facets, listed in Table~\ref{tab:facets}
and grouped into six families. Ten are drawn from the vocabulary of RF
systems engineering; the eleventh, \emph{objective}, is the
physical-layer security objective set, and is what makes the structure a
security taxonomy rather than a systems reference.

\guide{Facet selection is a methodological claim and reviewers will press
on it. Say how the eleven were derived---inductively from coding a corpus
of $n$ papers, or deductively from an existing systems-engineering
breakdown---and state the stopping criterion. ``It came from a whiteboard
sketch'' is true but not publishable; ``we coded $n$ sources until no new
facet appeared in the last $k$'' is both.}

\subsection{Relations as the Unit of Knowledge}
Because rows and columns are drawn from the same facet list, the matrix
is square and symmetric: the cell at (antenna, protocol) and the cell at
(protocol, antenna) denote the same relation read from opposite ends. We
author each relation once and derive its transpose, which reduces the
authoring surface from $121$ cells to $\binom{11}{2}=55$ distinct
relation types. The diagonal is excluded by definition: a facet crossed
with itself is a glossary entry, not a relation.

Each relation instance carries a set of concrete pairs, a maturity
assessment, free-text justification, and citations. A cell is therefore a
\emph{set}, not a value---a fact with direct consequences for the visual
encoding, developed in Section~\ref{sec:vis}.

\subsection{Evidence Density}
\label{sec:evidence}
Each relation carries a scalar $e \in [0,1]$ expressing how well that
relation is covered by the literature and by our own dataset. This
quantity, not relation identity, is what cell colour encodes.

\guide{\textbf{This subsection is the paper's methodological weak point
and you must not leave it as written.} A reviewer will ask exactly how $e$
is computed, and ``curatorial judgement'' will not survive. Pick one and
operationalise it:
(i)~a normalised count of distinct peer-reviewed sources meeting a stated
inclusion criterion;
(ii)~an ordinal rubric (0--4) with published anchor descriptions, coded by
two raters with a reported agreement statistic such as Cohen's $\kappa$ on
a sample of cells;
(iii)~a bibliometric measure computed from a frozen corpus.
Option (ii) is the cheapest defensible choice. Whatever you choose,
report inter-rater agreement---it converts the entire colour channel from
opinion into measurement, and it is the single highest-value experimental
investment in this paper.}

\section{Visual Design}
\label{sec:vis}

\subsection{Requirements}
The design is driven by five requirements derived from the structure
above and from feedback on an earlier single-view prototype:
\textbf{R1}, support lookup of an arbitrary facet pair;
\textbf{R2}, expose coverage so that absence of knowledge is visible
rather than indistinguishable from absence of display;
\textbf{R3}, preserve orientation while navigating a $121$-cell surface
that carries no cell text;
\textbf{R4}, remain legible under both light and dark presentation and in
greyscale print, since figures are extracted for publication;
\textbf{R5}, couple to the detail views so that a relation can be
inspected in its native representation.

\subsection{A Cyclic Axis}
The column order is not arbitrary. Reading around the surface:
protocol, band, modulation, antenna, propagation, noise, attack,
objective, mitigation, limitation, use-case, and back to protocol. This
is the countermeasure lifecycle---a design is allocated spectrum, encoded,
radiated, propagated, corrupted, attacked, defended, and limited, and
those limitations constrain the use-cases that drive the next design
generation.

Wrapping the axis is legitimate precisely because it closes. The
cylindrical presentation asserts that the ordering has no origin and no
terminus, which is a claim about the domain rather than a stylistic
choice; the arms-race framing is normally asserted in prose and never
shown. Highlighting a row lifts the far wall of the surface so that the
band is visibly continuous around the cylinder, making the closure
observable rather than merely stated.

\guide{Be prepared to abandon this. If the cyclic ordering cannot be
defended in review---and it is the load-bearing justification for the
entire 3D presentation---the honest fallback is the flat matrix, which
the tool already supports as a first-class mode. Write the paper so that
losing the cylinder costs you one subsection and one figure, not the
contribution.}

\subsection{Encoding}
Position identifies a cell: its row and column are given by where it is.
We therefore spend colour on what position cannot convey. Hue carries
facet family---six groups rather than eleven, a count within the range
over which categorical colour remains discriminable---and provides
orientation while the surface rotates. Luminance carries evidence density
on a single sequential ramp, which survives colour-vision deficiency and
greyscale reproduction (\textbf{R4}) and directs attention to where
substance lies (\textbf{R2}).

\subsubsection*{Rejected: identity as a colour blend}
An earlier design encoded cell identity as a blend of a row colour and a
column colour. We rejected it for three reasons, recorded here because
the reasoning generalises to any square-matrix encoding. First, colour
mixtures cannot be inverted perceptually: an observer shown a blend does
not recover its components, and shading across a curved surface perturbs
apparent hue further. Second, the scheme requires $121$ discriminable
blends generated from $11 \times 11$ parents that must themselves be
discriminable, and those two requirements are in direct tension. Third
and decisively, it spends the only free visual channel on information
that position already supplies, leaving a text-free surface with no
information scent---every cell equally featureless, and exploration
reduced to guesswork.

\guide{Design papers are strengthened, not weakened, by reporting
rejected alternatives. Keep this subsection. If you run the expert
walkthrough proposed in Section~VII, ask one participant to compare the
two encodings directly; a single comparative observation converts this
from argument into evidence.}

\subsection{Interaction}
Cells carry no text; selecting one opens a modal containing the pair
list, evidence, citations, and links into the detail views. Removing text
from the surface eliminates glyph distortion under curvature entirely.

Orientation (\textbf{R3}) is maintained by a crosshair: hovering or
focusing a cell illuminates the full row band and column stripe and
prints the pair name in a fixed readout that never moves. This is
deliberately not header-tracing. Curvature actively degrades tracing,
because column lines converge toward the silhouette; the fixed readout
sidesteps the task rather than competing with it.

Column labels persist around the entire circumference, with blur and
opacity ramped by depth, so that a label on the far side is present but
unresolvable until it rotates forward. Nothing is hidden; resolution is
deferred.

Finally, an unroll control interpolates the cylinder into a flat grid
with identical data and identical cells. The cylinder supports
orientation and the argument about cyclicity; the grid supports
comparison of distant columns, which curvature makes impossible.

\section{System Architecture}

\subsection{One Graph, Four Projections}
All four views are reads of a single entity graph. Nodes are facet
members; edges are relation instances. Critically, graph edges are
\emph{derived} from the authored relation records rather than stored
separately---in an earlier iteration, every integration defect we
encountered arose from two tables that were independently correct but had
drifted apart.

The four views are: \textbf{Home}, the facet matrix described above;
\textbf{TRANSEC}, a ground-to-orbit scene with emitters, orbital regimes,
and a draggable observer that reports whether a link is exposed,
detectable as energy only, or hidden; \textbf{COMSEC}, a device-level
scene in which selecting an attack illuminates its kill chain across
terminal, modem, cryptographic unit, teleport, and backhaul; and
\textbf{Signal Lab}, a waveform laboratory presenting constellation,
spectrum, eye, waterfall, and spreading views over a tested
signal-processing core, with adversary controls for barrage,
partial-band, and follower jamming.

Coupling is the point (\textbf{R5}): a cell in Home opens the
corresponding kill chain in COMSEC, and a modulation in either opens in
Signal Lab with the relevant scheme loaded.

\guide{Say explicitly why COMSEC is present, since it overlaps SPARTA's
territory. The defensible framing: SPARTA enumerates techniques but does
not show them propagating through concrete equipment. The visualization is
the contribution; the COMSEC taxonomy is not.}

\subsection{Provenance and Reproducibility}
External knowledge bases are ingested offline and their normalised output
is committed to the repository with per-record provenance and retrieval
date. Nothing is fetched at runtime. Three reasons motivate this:
a reviewer must be able to rebuild the artefact and obtain the same
matrix; a static deployment has no server on which to proxy
cross-origin requests or rate limits; and licensing attribution remains
auditable.

The mapping from SPARTA techniques to signal facets is hand-curated, not
automated. The two schemes operate at different levels of abstraction,
and most mission-level techniques have no physical-layer correlate; an
automated join produces plausible-looking but spurious entries, which are
worse than absent ones because they are invisible.

\guide{State the licence position for each external source explicitly in
a table---name, licence, what you import, what you do not. For SPARTA in
particular, confirm terms before submission: cite technique identifiers
and link outward, author original descriptions, and do not mirror text.}

\subsection{Implementation}
The tool is a client-side TypeScript application. The three-dimensional
scenes use WebGL; the laboratory views are Canvas2D renderers over a
signal-processing core with unit tests and no user-interface coupling.
All application state, including the selected facet pair, is encoded in
the URL, so that any view state is deep-linkable and directly citable.
The artefact deploys as static files.

\section{Usage Scenarios}

\guide{Two scenarios is the right number for five pages; three if you cut
elsewhere. Each should be a task a real analyst has, not a feature tour,
and each should end with an answer the reader could not have obtained
from the sources cited in Section~II.}

\subsection{Locating a Research Gap}
An analyst opens the Home view and observes that relations involving the
inter-satellite-link facet are uniformly low in evidence density, while
relations among band, modulation, and objective are dense. The surface has
functioned as a map of the literature rather than only as a reference: the
sparse region is a research agenda. Selecting one such relation reveals
the small number of citations backing it.

\subsection{GNSS Spoofing}
\needdata{} A user selects the GNSS civil signal and steps through
authentic, meaconed, and synthetic conditions in Signal Lab, observing
the correlation peak split and drag; enabling the navigation-message
authentication mitigation restores it. Crossing back to Home, the
objective row shows why the civil signal is structurally exposed---it has
no user uplink, and therefore no challenge-response path---while the
military signal's split-spectrum waveform is visible directly in the
spectrum panel.

\guide{This scenario is the strongest thing in the paper because it
exercises the relational structure, the waveform library, and a live
real-world problem simultaneously. Consider making it Section~VI in full
and demoting the gap-finding scenario to a paragraph.}

\section{Evaluation Plan}

\guide{\textbf{A tool paper without evaluation is rejected.} Decide now
which of the following you will actually run, because the choice shapes
what you must instrument before the study, not after. In descending order
of credibility per unit of effort:}

\subsection{Classroom Deployment}
Deploy the tool in a course covering satellite or wireless security and
collect pre- and post-instruction comprehension measures, with a
comparison condition using conventional materials. This is the most
achievable design and yields the strongest claim.

\subsection{Expert Walkthrough}
Structured think-aloud sessions with practitioners from the SIGINT,
electronic-warfare, and satellite-operations communities, coded for
breakdowns in orientation, encoding interpretation, and cross-view
navigation. Six to eight participants is conventional.

\subsection{Task-Based Study}
Timed lookup and comparison tasks with and without the tool. This is also
the natural setting for the one controlled comparison the visual design
demands: cylinder versus unrolled grid, on tasks requiring comparison of
distant columns.

\guide{Run that cylinder-versus-grid comparison even if you run nothing
else. It is the empirical answer to the objection your central design
choice invites, and if the cylinder loses on comparison tasks but wins on
orientation and engagement, that is a publishable and honest result---not
a failure.}

\subsection{Case-Study Validation}
Reproduce documented incidents end to end and show that the model
accounts for them. This is cheap, requires no participants, and
demonstrates coverage.

\section{Limitations}

\textbf{Coverage is bounded by curation.} The taxonomy reflects the
literature the authors reached. Evidence density therefore measures our
corpus, not the field, and must be reported as such.

\textbf{Occlusion.} The cylinder shows roughly half the matrix at once.
The unrolled mode mitigates this, but the default view does withhold
information, and the far wall is deliberately illegible.

\textbf{The cyclic ordering is argued, not derived.} It is a defensible
reading of the countermeasure lifecycle, not a result. Alternative
orderings exist and would produce a different adjacency structure.

\textbf{Symmetry is an assumption.} Treating (A,~B) and (B,~A) as one
relation presumes the relationship is not directional. For most facet
pairs this holds; for attack and mitigation it is arguable.

\guide{Adding a fifth limitation you have actually encountered---rather
than one you anticipate---raises reviewer confidence measurably. The
camera-tilt finding from the prototype is a genuine candidate: at a steep
viewing angle the offset between a row's silhouette and its front face
exceeded one row height and the display was misread. It is a small,
concrete, honest observation about curved matrix layouts.}

\section{Conclusion}
We have presented a relational taxonomy of RF signal security in which
the unit of knowledge is the crossing of two signal-domain facets rather
than a single property, together with an interactive instrument that
renders those crossings on a cyclic surface and couples them to three
detail views over a shared entity graph. By encoding evidence density
rather than identity, the artefact serves simultaneously as a reference
and as a map of where knowledge is absent.

\guide{Close on the gap map, not on the visualization. ``Here is what
nobody has studied'' is a stronger and more durable claim than ``here is
a new way to draw a matrix,'' and it is the claim your encoding uniquely
supports.}
